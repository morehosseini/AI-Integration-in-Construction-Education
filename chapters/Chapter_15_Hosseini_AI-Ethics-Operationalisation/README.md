# Chapter 15 — Operationalising AI Ethics in Construction: Principles, Governance and Tools

**Authors:** M. Reza Hosseini, Abdolmajid Erfani, Fahimeh Abedi, Deniz Besiktepe, Heng Li, Miroslaw J. Skibniewski  
**Part:** Part IV: Capstone  
**Status:** Manuscript delivered to the publisher, November 2026; in production

## About this chapter

This chapter provides a practical framework for operationalising artificial intelligence (AI) ethics in construction, translating the professional and ethical foundations established in Chapter 5 into systematic organisational practice. It frames ethical AI not as an external compliance burden but as an extension of construction professionals’ existing duties. Firstly, it articulates seven core principles of AI-extended ethics—safety, reliability, fairness, privacy, transparency, accountability and environmental responsibility—consolidated into four operational pillars under the human-centred, ethical and responsible AI (HCER-AI) model. Secondly, it sets out organisational governance for ethical AI use, comparing centralised, decentralised and hybrid governance structures and detailing life cycle governance from selection and procurement to decommissioning, alongside the roles construction engineers assume as AI deployers. Thirdly, it maps technological tools for bias detection, explainability, privacy preservation, accountability and environmental monitoring to each principle. Finally, it examines the role of government in supporting ethical AI deployment through regulation, certification and softer enabling mechanisms. By supplying concrete instruments, including a vendor-evaluation checklist, the chapter equips construction organisations to embed ethical AI within existing roles, workflows and decision making.
