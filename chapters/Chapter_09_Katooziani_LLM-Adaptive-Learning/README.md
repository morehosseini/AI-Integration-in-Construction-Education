# Chapter 9 — Large Language Models (LLMs) for Adaptive and Personalised Learning in Construction Education

**Authors:** Ali Katooziani, Masoud Gheisari, Idris Jeelani  
**Part:** Part II: Teaching, Curriculum and Professional Formation  
**Status:** Manuscript delivered to the publisher, November 2026; in production

## About this chapter

This chapter explores how Large Language Models (LLMs) can bring personalised learning to construction education. Students and workers in this field arrive with many different backgrounds, learning speeds and language skills, yet current training remains largely one size fits all. We first trace the idea of tailoring instruction to each learner, from early constructivist theory to modern adaptive software. We next explain how LLMs, such as GPT-4, work and why their natural-language dialogue, instant feedback and multilingual support make them strong candidates for personalised tutoring. We review applications of ChatGPT and similar tools in hazard recognition, student writing and design-stage hazard identification, alongside examples from engineering and medical education. These studies illustrate potential uses, with outcomes dependent on their design and context. Benefits include round-the-clock coaching, content that adjusts to individuals’ gaps and quick translation of complex terms. Risks include inaccurate or biased answers, misuse by learners and added cost. The chapter offers practical guidance on model fine-tuning, teacher oversight, ethical policies and assessment redesign. We conclude that, with careful integration, LLMs can shift construction education from fixed lectures to flexible pathways that respect learner diversity and better prepare professionals for an evolving industry.
