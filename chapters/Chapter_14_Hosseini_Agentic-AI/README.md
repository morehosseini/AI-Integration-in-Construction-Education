# Chapter 14 — Agentic AI in Construction: Frameworks, Use Cases and Practical Governance

**Authors:** M. Reza Hosseini, Mohamad Kassem, Ruoxin Xiong, Bree Trevena  
**Part:** Part III: Data, Infrastructure and Applied AI Capabilities  
**Status:** Manuscript delivered to the publisher, November 2026; in production

## About this chapter

Artificial intelligence (AI) agents represent a fundamental shift from passive software to dynamic, autonomous collaborators that can perceive their environment, reason through problems and act on the world. By connecting Large Language Models (LLMs) to external tools and data sources, agentic AI transforms static applications into goal-directed systems that can plan, execute multi-step workflows and adapt to unexpected challenges in real time. In construction, an industry characterised by fragmented processes, persistent coordination failures, skilled labour shortages, and chronic cost and schedule overruns, the stakes of this shift are especially high. This chapter introduces the core architecture of AI agents, such as goal-directed behaviour, perception, planning, tool use and varying degrees of autonomy, and explains how these differ from conventional predictive or rules-based systems. It examines how single agents and multi-agent arrangements integrate with BIM, IoT and digital twins across the construction life cycle, and addresses the governance questions that matter most in practice: human oversight, accountability, data quality, interoperability and the conditions under which autonomous action is, and is not, appropriate. Worked cases show where these systems add genuine value and where professional judgement must remain central.
