# Chapter 6 — AI in Construction: Identifying Competencies and Analysing Teaching Methods

**Authors:** Kàtia Gaspar, Núria Forcada  
**Part:** Part II: Teaching, Curriculum and Professional Formation  
**Status:** Manuscript delivered to the publisher, November 2026; in production

## About this chapter

Construction has been slow to adopt new technologies, although artificial intelligence (AI) is set to revolutionise its practices. With the potential to automate tasks, optimise resource allocation and improve project management, AI can transform construction workflows. However, the integration of AI into the construction industry requires a corresponding shift in education, equipping professionals with the necessary skills to leverage these technologies. Traditional educational frameworks often focus on research-driven AI applications, leaving gaps in real-world hands-on learning. To address these gaps, this chapter explores the growing relevance of AI competencies in construction education, emphasising practical, industry-oriented training. It also examines teaching methodologies, such as problem-based learning; flipped classroom models; blended learning; experimental learning through simulations; gamification; and interdisciplinary collaborative learning. In particular, it compares the effectiveness of these methods in offering explorative, interactive or immersive experiences, depending on the specific construction application. This analysis highlights how different approaches can foster deeper understanding, critical thinking and the development of practical AI skills among architecture, engineering and construction (AEC) professionals. The findings suggest that adopting these methods would enable education institutions to more effectively equip future construction professionals with the skills needed to harness the capabilities of AI within the built environment.
