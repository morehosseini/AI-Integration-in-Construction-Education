# Chapter 13 — Digital Twins as Pedagogical Tools for Construction 4.0

**Authors:** Jenn McArthur  
**Part:** Part III: Data, Infrastructure and Applied AI Capabilities  
**Status:** Manuscript delivered to the publisher, November 2026; in production

## About this chapter

Digital twins (DTs) play an increasing role in the architecture, engineering and construction (AEC) sector with the emergence of Construction 4.0 and provide a means to bring the construction site ‘into’ the classroom, thus enabling experiential activities. This chapter presents a framework for experiential learning using digital twins, mapping learning activity types to Kolb’s experiential learning cycle and identifying associated learning outcomes based on Bloom’s taxonomy. Using the Digital Twin Capabilities Periodic Table, opportunities for experiential learning through using the framework are identified for each of the capability categories: data services, integration, intelligence, user experience, management and trustworthiness, with examples provided for each. Challenges in digital twin adoption in the classroom are related primarily to a lack of available and appropriate digital twins and to faculty capacity. The former can be overcome through the use of digital shadows, ideally supported by simulation environments, to permit multi-user simultaneous engagement with the twin or lab-based physical twins constructed specifically for pedagogical use. The latter can be supported through the development of instructional guides and reference materials to support digital twin classroom deployment. Finally, a case study is presented for an Emerging Technologies course, showcasing various uses of digital twins/shadows to support experiential learning in the context of Construction 4.0.
