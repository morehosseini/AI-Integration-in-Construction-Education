# Chapter 4 — When Technical Outputs No Longer Demonstrate Competence: A Capability Framework for AI-Enabled Built Environment Education

**Authors:** Mohamad Kassem, Miroslaw J. Skibniewski, Abdulmalik Badamasi, M. Reza Hosseini  
**Part:** Part I: Foundations  
**Status:** Manuscript delivered to the publisher, November 2026; in production

## About this chapter

Artificial intelligence (AI), particularly large language models (LLMs), is transforming built environment education by enabling students to automate the generation of increasingly sophisticated designs, analyses, models and recommendations with unprecedented speed. This chapter argues that the principal educational challenge is not automation itself, but the growing separation between technically plausible outputs and the professional judgement those outputs have traditionally been taken to evidence. It conceptualises this divergence as the output–judgement gap. Building on the literature on AI in education, professional judgement and built environment education, the chapter proposes four complementary capabilities—fundamentals, counterfactual reasoning, critical verification and capability formation—and introduces the non-delegable boundary to explain why responsibility for determining whether AI-generated outputs should inform professional action remains irreducibly human. It translates the framework into principles for curriculum design, assessment and AI integration in built environment programs.
